## What

Build a corpus for the prohibition and review whether the repository itself ever makes a
single-food disease claim, and whether the prohibited boundary is determinable by an adopter.

## Why

The prohibition has no worked violating example, so the review had a concrete question attached:
is that a documentation gap, or does the corpus fail to demonstrate the prohibition clearly enough
to release?

## Notes

The centrepiece of the corpus is mechanical rather than argued. Scanning the whole nutrition domain
for food names returns five lines; the same scan over the rest of the repository returns one. The
repository does not make the claim it prohibits, and that is checkable rather than asserted.

Disposition ESTABLISHABLE. The missing example was classified a **documentation opportunity, not a
defect** — an adopter can determine the boundary from Standard 14 R3 and Standard 37 R1/R5, and
examples should demonstrate semantics rather than create them.

Explicitly considered and **rejected**: an invariant that every prohibition must carry a worked
negative example. Three other prohibitions lack one. Recorded in ST-09 so a later reader cannot
reconstruct that rule from the finding.
