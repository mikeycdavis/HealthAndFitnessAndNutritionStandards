## What

Verify the certification candidate against the recorded procedure, then remove the
`|| [ $? -eq 4 ]` allowance from CI, move `VERSION` off `1.0.0-dev`, re-run the full gate, and tag
`v1.0.0`.

## Why

Adoption must pin an immutable release. Until the tag exists there is nothing an adopter may
legitimately pin to.

## Notes

**Blocked on:** explicit authorization to perform the release. ST-05 is complete — the four
attestations are validly recorded and `check` reports `COMPLIANT` at exit 0 — and that is precisely
the moment this item is most likely to be started by mistake. A green verdict is not authorization.
Changing `VERSION`, removing CI's exit-4 allowance, and tagging `v1.0.0` are outward-facing and
irreversible, and each needs to be asked for.

The verification order is itself the control, and it is recorded in
[`artifacts/release-review/dispositions.md`](../../release-review/dispositions.md):

> diff against `7f59f8b` → explain every consequential change → verify evidence-chain integrity →
> compare per-rule states → compare verdict → release mechanics

The verdict is an output, not the release evidence. A `COMPLIANT` verdict is not evidence that an
unexplained diff is acceptable, and a matching predicted state is not either.

Six post-baseline commits are now known and must be explained rather than assumed benign:
`cc0c6de` (BACKLOG.md only), `6c2f447` (dispositions.md only), `6352108` (the backlog and its
certification record), `ad6bdcb` (attestations recorded — **do not certify this commit**), `7b650f2`
(the withdrawal), and the commit recording the valid attestations. The last three move verdict inputs
and are the reason the procedure exists. The baseline does not advance for any of them.

`ad6bdcb` is the worked demonstration that a candidate can match every predicted per-rule state and
still be uncertifiable. Explain the provenance of a verdict-affecting input, not only its shape.
