## What

Adoption testing covers policy files, the CI workflow, branch-protection assumptions, `CODEOWNERS`,
attestation instructions, version and migration metadata, and agent instructions **before**
descriptive documentation.

## Why

Incorrect operational instructions cause invalid actions. Incorrect descriptive prose mostly causes
misunderstanding.

## Notes

The reason is evidence, not architecture. Three defects found late in 1.0.0 certification were all
on surfaces someone acts on:

1. `project-policy.yml`'s attestations comment named a rule that is not attestable, which would
   have sent a certifier to trigger `BLOCKED_BY_INVARIANT`.
2. The README's verdict table stated a false definition of `COMPLIANT`, disproved by a dry run.
3. The certification procedure itself was never written onto the surface it governs — it existed
   only in conversation until `6c2f447`.

The drift test that existed covered `PROJECT.md` and `CHANGELOG.md` — files that describe — and
caught none of them. A fourth guard was considered after the third instance and **rejected**: the
general property ("does this actionable instruction cause a person to do the correct thing") has no
reliable mechanical form, and a guard is justified by a mechanical property, not by a count of
examples. What found all three was using the repository as its operator would.

Requirement 8 of EP-03. Both of the first two are recorded in
[CHANGELOG.md](../../../CHANGELOG.md).
