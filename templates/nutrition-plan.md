# Nutrition plan — <person or purpose>

<One paragraph: what this plan is for and over what period.>

## Targets

<Energy, protein, and fiber, each with the basis it was derived from.

Mark values: `2,300 kcal (estimated)`, `140 g protein (estimated from body mass)`,
`30 g fiber (estimated)`. An estimate and a measurement look identical once written down, and
precision gets read from the number of digits rather than from the source.

Rate of change, where the plan changes body mass: give a range with what makes it vary, and say what
will be watched to tell whether it is working. Never a promised figure — a promised rate will be
wrong, and being wrong that way teaches the person that the approach failed when it was the promise
that failed.

Energy is one target among several. It is listed first because it is the one that crowds out the
others when it is the only one written down.>

## Adequacy

<How this plan meets nutritional needs beyond energy.

Name the nutrients this plan's own structure puts at risk, and say how each is covered or that it is
not. "Balanced" is not adequacy consideration — the useful form is specific enough to be wrong.

Risk rises with lower total energy, with each excluded food group, with repetitive eating patterns,
and with life stage and circumstance. Say which of those apply here.

Where a plan structurally cannot supply something, supplementation is a reasonable answer and belongs
in the plan. Recommending supplementation to correct a suspected deficiency is a clinical matter,
not a wellness one.>

## Restrictions and Context

<What is known about what this person can and will eat.

  Safety-critical   allergies, coeliac disease, other medically required exclusions
  Medical           conditions and medications with dietary implications
  Ethical/religious vegetarian and vegan practice, observance, fasting periods
  Intolerance       digestive intolerances, foods and textures they will not eat
  Practical         budget, cooking access and skill, time, availability, who else eats this
  Cultural          food traditions, staples, what a normal meal is

Only the first class is unsafe to violate. The rest determine whether the plan is followed at all,
and a plan that respects only the safety-critical class produces guidance nobody uses.

Where none are known, write "None known" — a different claim from "none". The default assumption
that there are no restrictions is the one that causes harm when it is wrong.>

## Approach

<How the targets are met in practice, and why this approach is one this person can sustain.

Sustainability is not willpower. It is: fits their life, includes foods they like, tolerates an
imperfect day, survives travel and other people, and needs effort proportionate to its benefit.

Describe foods by what they contribute — protein, fibre, micronutrients, satiety, cost, convenience,
enjoyment. Never as good or bad, clean or cheating, earned or owed. Shame does not improve adherence;
it predicts concealment and abandonment.

Where this plan will not be adhered to, the plan is the first thing to examine.>

## Review

<When this will be reviewed and against what.

Body weight is read as a trend over weeks, using an average rather than individual readings. Day to
day it moves on water, glycogen, sodium, gut contents, and cycle — routinely by more than a week of
actual fat change.

Expenditure falls as body mass falls. When the rate slows, that is a decision point — adjust intake,
adjust activity, or accept the slower rate — not an automatic reason to cut further. Cutting further
at each slowdown is how crash dieting is arrived at one step at a time.>
