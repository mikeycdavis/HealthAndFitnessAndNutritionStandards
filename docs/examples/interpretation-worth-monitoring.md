# Interpretation — blood pressure 148/94, 2026-03-14

A single elevated reading taken under conditions that make it unreliable. The useful output here is
not an interpretation — it is a better measurement.

*Fictional illustration. Not a record of any real person, and not guidance to anyone.*

## Observation

Blood pressure 148/94 mmHg (measured), home upper-arm cuff, 2026-03-14, approximately 14:20.

Single reading. No prior home readings recorded.

## Context

Reported: the reading was taken within a minute or two of arriving home, after carrying shopping up
two flights of stairs. Coffee about forty minutes earlier. The person describes themselves as
"stressed about work at the moment".

No symptoms reported — no headache, no visual disturbance, no chest discomfort, no breathlessness.

## Measurement Quality

**Poor, and this is the dominant fact about this reading.**

Validated home upper-arm cuff, which is a good instrument. But the protocol that reference ranges
assume was not followed: five minutes seated and at rest beforehand, back supported, feet flat, arm
at heart height, no caffeine or exertion in the preceding thirty minutes, and — importantly — an
average of two or three readings a minute apart, not a single one.

Recent stair climbing and caffeine both plausibly raise a reading by an amount comparable to the
elevation being discussed here. A single reading also cannot separate a real elevation from
white-coat-type response or ordinary variability, which is substantial for blood pressure.

## Baseline

None known. No prior home readings, and no clinic readings available.

This is a first data point, and it begins to establish a baseline rather than being read against one.

## Medications and Contextual Factors

None known. No medications reported. Worth establishing, since several classes affect blood pressure
directly.

## Uncertainty

High, and the uncertainty is mostly about the measurement rather than about the person.

Candidate explanations, none clearly ahead:

- **Measurement conditions.** Exertion and caffeine, plus a single unaveraged reading. This alone
  could account for the whole elevation.
- **Situational response.** Reported work stress; blood pressure is responsive to it.
- **Genuinely elevated blood pressure.** Entirely possible, and not distinguishable from the above
  on this evidence.

What would distinguish them: a proper series of readings, taken to protocol, over a week or two. That
is a cheap test and it resolves most of this.

## Escalation Tier

**worth monitoring**

Not because the number is reassuring — it is not — but because this measurement cannot support a
conclusion, and the right next step is a better measurement rather than an interpretation of a poor
one.

What to do, over the next one to two weeks:

- Sit quietly for five minutes first, back supported, feet flat, arm supported at heart height.
- No caffeine, exercise, or smoking for thirty minutes beforehand.
- Take two or three readings a minute apart and record all of them.
- Twice daily, roughly the same times — morning before medication or breakfast, and evening.

Then look at the average, not at any single reading.

If that average remains elevated, that is a pattern rather than a reading, and it moves to *worth
discussing with a professional*. Bring the full record.

Sooner than that, and regardless of the numbers: severe headache, visual disturbance, chest pain,
breathlessness, or neurological symptoms warrant prompt evaluation.
