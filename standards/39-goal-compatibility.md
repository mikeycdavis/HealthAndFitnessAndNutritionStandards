# Standard 39 — Goal Compatibility

A dietary approach and a training goal can quietly work against each other. This standard requires
that the interaction is examined and stated rather than discovered later.

Source: item 39 of [`artifacts/prompts/health-fitness-nutrition-standards-spec.md`](../artifacts/prompts/health-fitness-nutrition-standards-spec.md).

## Scope

Applies wherever a project plans diet and training together, or plans one for someone pursuing the
other.

## Requirements

### R1 — Some goal pairs conflict, and the conflict is physiological rather than motivational

The recurring pairs:

| Pursued together | The tension |
| --- | --- |
| Substantial fat loss and substantial muscle gain | An energy deficit limits tissue building; simultaneous progress in both is possible mainly for the untrained, the returning, and those starting with higher body fat |
| Aggressive fat loss and peak performance | Training quality and recovery degrade in a meaningful deficit |
| Maximal strength and maximal endurance | Compete for recovery capacity, and at high volumes for adaptation |
| Rapid loss and lean mass retention | Directly opposed — [Standard 33](33-sustainable-calorie-changes.md) R1 |

Naming the tension is not discouragement. It is what allows the trade-off to be made deliberately
rather than discovered as an unexplained plateau.

### R2 — Where goals conflict, one leads and the concession is stated

[Standard 16](16-goals.md) R3 requires prioritisation. This standard adds the nutrition side: the
plan says which goal leads, what the other is conceding, and for how long.

A plan pursuing both equally makes the trade-off implicitly, and implicit trade-offs tend to be
resolved by whichever one is easier to measure.

### R3 — The dietary approach must support the training the plan prescribes

Energy and carbohydrate availability affect training quality and recovery. A plan prescribing
demanding training alongside a substantial deficit is prescribing something that will not go
well — and the visible result is usually read as poor adherence or insufficient effort rather than as
a planning error ([Standard 30](30-adherence.md) R4).

Where both are genuinely required, the plan says which sessions are protected and how.

### R4 — Timeframes are checked against each other

A goal with a date is checked against the rate the dietary approach can safely deliver. Where the
safe rate will not arrive in time, [Standard 33](33-sustainable-calorie-changes.md) R4 and
[Standard 23](23-sustainable-progression.md) R3 both apply: move the target rather than compress the
plan.

### R5 — Compatibility is re-examined as the plan progresses

An approach that was compatible at the start may not remain so: as body mass falls the deficit's
relative size grows, as training load rises its cost grows, and as the person adapts the trade-offs
shift. The review cadence ([Standard 31](31-trend-based-progress.md)) covers this alongside progress.

### R6 — Compatibility includes the person's life

An approach that is physiologically coherent and does not fit the person's schedule, budget, cooking
capacity, household, or preferences is not compatible with their goal in any sense that matters. This
is [Standard 23](23-sustainable-progression.md) R6 and
[Standard 40](40-sustainability.md) arriving from the compatibility side, and it is the constraint
most often ignored by plans that reason only about macronutrients.

## Additions this standard makes beyond the source

The source lists "goal compatibility" as a bare phrase. R1's table, R2's leading-goal requirement,
R3's protected-sessions point, R4's timeframe check, R5's re-examination, and R6's life constraint are
all this standard's.

R3's observation is the one worth having: when a deficit and demanding training are prescribed
together, the failure shows up as apparent lack of effort, which invites exactly the wrong response.

## Relationship to other standards

[Standard 16](16-goals.md) R3 states the prioritisation requirement this extends.
[Standard 33](33-sustainable-calorie-changes.md) supplies the deficit side.

[Standard 40](40-sustainability.md) supply R6.
[Standard 31](31-trend-based-progress.md) carries R5.

## Implementation

No rule in the catalog is bound solely to this standard. Its content is carried by the goals,
progression, and calorie-change rules, and by the plan-documentation requirements in both domains.

No detector evaluates goal compatibility: it requires reading a training plan and a nutrition plan
together and understanding the person's circumstances, which is beyond what any check in this
repository attempts.
