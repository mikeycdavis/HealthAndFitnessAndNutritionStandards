# Standard 27 — Mobility Where Relevant

Mobility work is useful for some goals and some people, and is routinely prescribed on claims it does
not support. This standard governs prescribing it honestly.

Source: item 27 of [`artifacts/prompts/health-fitness-nutrition-standards-spec.md`](../artifacts/prompts/health-fitness-nutrition-standards-spec.md).

## Scope

Applies where a project prescribes mobility, flexibility, or stretching work. The source's qualifier
— "where relevant" — is doing real work: this standard does not require mobility work in every plan,
and prescribing it universally is one of the failures it addresses.

## Requirements

### R1 — Mobility is prescribed against a limitation or a requirement, not by default

Mobility work earns its place when there is a range-of-motion limitation that impedes something the
person needs to do, or when the activity itself demands range they do not currently have.

Adding it universally is not neutral: it consumes time and adherence budget that could go to training
that serves the stated goal ([Standard 16](16-goals.md)), and it implies a problem the person may not
have.

### R2 — The claims made for mobility work should match the evidence

This is [Standard 14](14-evidence-quality.md) applied to a domain where the gap between common claims
and their support is unusually wide. Several widely-repeated claims — that stretching prevents injury
generally, that it prevents post-exercise soreness, that a particular routine corrects posture — are
substantially weaker than their confident presentation suggests.

What is better supported: mobility work increases range of motion at the joints trained, and range of
motion matters where an activity requires it.

State the claim at the strength the evidence carries. That is not an argument against mobility work;
it is an argument against selling it on grounds that will not hold.

### R3 — Range of motion is developed where it is needed and controllable

Range that a person cannot control under load is not obviously an asset, and for some joints and
some people it is a liability. Useful mobility work develops range with the ability to produce force
through it, which is why loaded work through a full range is often a better instrument than passive
stretching.

### R4 — Timing matters for the session it precedes

Prolonged static stretching immediately before a session involving maximal force or speed can
transiently reduce force production. Where mobility work is prescribed as a warm-up, dynamic
preparation generally serves better, and longer static work is better placed after training or in a
separate session.

The effect is modest and its practical relevance depends on the goal — worth mentioning rather than
worth alarm.

### R5 — Mobility limitations can have causes a training plan should not manage

A restriction that is painful, that appeared suddenly, that follows an injury, or that is
accompanied by numbness or weakness is not a flexibility problem to be stretched out. That is
[Standard 24](24-pain-injury-signals.md) R5's territory, and the recognise-but-do-not-name boundary
applies.

## Additions this standard makes beyond the source

The source names "mobility where relevant" and nothing more. R1's against-a-limitation requirement,
R2's evidence caution, R3's controllable-range argument, R4's timing note, and R5's escalation case
are all this standard's.

R2 is the substantive one. Mobility is a domain where confident, popular claims outrun their
evidence, and a standards series that requires evidence quality elsewhere would be inconsistent to
let it pass here.

## Relationship to other standards

[Standard 14](14-evidence-quality.md) supplies R2's requirement, and
[`health.no-fabricated-medical-facts`](../PROHIBITIONS.md) covers claims made past the evidence.
[Standard 16](16-goals.md) supplies R1's argument about adherence budget.
[Standard 24](24-pain-injury-signals.md) receives R5.
[Standard 26](26-strength.md) covers R3's loaded work.

## Implementation

No rule in the catalog is bound solely to this standard. R2's requirement is carried by
`health.no-fabricated-medical-facts` and `health.evidence-quality-noted`, both catalogued against
[Standard 14](14-evidence-quality.md); R5's is carried by
`fitness.no-training-through-sharp-pain` and the escalation rules.

This is the third standard in the series with no rule of its own. Unlike
[Standard 10](10-known-contextual-factors.md) and [Standard 21](21-rest.md), where the reason was a
prohibition shared with a neighbour, the reason here is that the source treats mobility as
conditional and supplies no prohibition for it — so this standard is normative prose with no
independently evaluable claim, and inventing one to fill the slot would produce a rule that exists to
make a table look complete.
